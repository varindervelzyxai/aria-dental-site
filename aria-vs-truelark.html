Aria vs TrueLark: an honest 2026 comparison

TrueLark covers dental, medspa, and other verticals; Aria is dental-only. Honest comparison on focus, integrations, pricing, voice, and which to pick when.

TrueLark (formerly CallBird) is a multi-vertical AI receptionist platform — they serve dental, medspa, salon, veterinary, and a handful of other appointment-based businesses. Aria is dental-only. That's the central tradeoff in this comparison: breadth vs. depth.

Bias disclosure

We make Aria. We've tried to be fair — based on public information about TrueLark as of May 7, 2026, plus customer reports we've heard during sales conversations. If anything's outdated or inaccurate, email hello@ariadental.ai.

Quick verdict

If you operate a single-vertical dental practice (or DSO) and want a vendor whose every feature, prompt, and integration is tuned for dentistry, Aria. If you operate a multi-concept business that includes dental plus other verticals — or you're a DSO already on TrueLark for medspa offerings — TrueLark's cross-vertical platform earns its keep.

Vertical focus

Aria is a dental product. Every prompt template, every PMS integration, every default appointment type, every insurance flow is tuned for dental practices. We don't have to ship medspa-specific features, vet appointment types, or salon stylist routing — and we don't have to maintain prompt logic for industries we don't serve. That focus shows up in things like procedure code mapping (CDT codes by default), insurance flows (X12 270/271 with dental-specific payer aliases), and patient context (recall types, treatment plan stages).

TrueLark's cross-vertical platform is impressive engineering — it's harder to build a system that flexes across dental, medspa, and vet than to build one that nails dental. But if you're a dental practice, you don't get a discount on the engineering you don't use. You get a system that handles your call type well, plus whatever cross-vertical generality the platform inherits.

Pricing approach

Aria publishes a price range and operates on monthly contracts. TrueLark is quote-only and tends toward annual commitments, especially for multi-location and DSO accounts. The DSO motion — TrueLark's strongest segment — favors annual deals at negotiated rates that single-location practices won't get. If you're a 1–3 location practice, Aria's transparent monthly pricing is usually easier to start with.

PMS integrations

Both products integrate with Open Dental, Dentrix, and Eaglesoft. TrueLark's PMS coverage is broad and well-tested across the multi-vertical roster. Aria's depth — what we mean by "integration" — is purpose-built for the dental call types that matter: appointment write-back including procedure mapping, real-time slot conflict detection, family/guarantor relationship handling.

Voice quality

Both products use modern voice AI stacks. In our internal testing, dental-specific call flows (insurance verification mid-call, paired appointments like ortho consults, multi-provider scheduling) feel more natively handled in Aria. Cross-vertical flows (chat that tries to book a yoga class then a tooth cleaning in the same session) aren't a real test case for either of us — they don't happen in dental practices.

HIPAA & security

Both vendors will sign a BAA. TrueLark, as a multi-vertical platform serving healthcare-adjacent verticals (medspa, vet) and consumer verticals (salon, fitness), maintains compliance posture across all of them. Aria's posture is documented at /security. For practices whose security review is "show me the BAA, the subprocessor list, and the encryption policy," both vendors should be willing — push.

Setup time

Aria's median single-location setup is 2–7 days. TrueLark's reported setup, especially for DSOs, runs 2–4 weeks. If your practice has a hard deadline (lost a front-desk lead, summer rush, opening a new location), Aria is faster. If you're rolling out across 12 locations on a planned schedule, TrueLark's longer timeline reflects real cross-location complexity that has to happen anyway.

Customer support

TrueLark has the more established customer success org — dedicated CSMs for larger accounts, formal ticket SLAs, professional services for complex rollouts. Aria is owner-operated; during onboarding most escalations route to Varinder personally. The right answer depends on what you want: documented SLAs and a formal CSM, or a number to text who has full system context.

When TrueLark is the better choice

You operate a multi-concept business that includes dental plus other verticals — TrueLark's cross-vertical platform consolidates the vendor list. You're already on TrueLark for another vertical. You're a DSO with 30+ locations and want a long-term enterprise vendor relationship with formal CSM coverage. Web chat is a major patient acquisition channel for you and you want best-in-class chat handling.

When Aria is the better choice

You're a dental-only practice or DSO and want a dental-focused vendor. You're 1–30 locations and want fast deployment. You want monthly contracts, published pricing, and a self-serve admin panel. You want hands-on Southern California-based support during the first 90 days. You're on Open Dental, Dentrix, or Eaglesoft and want deep PMS write-back tuned for dental call types.

Three scenarios

Scenario 1

Solo dental practice in Newport Beach, just opened a second location, wants AI receptionist live in two weeks.

→ Aria. Faster setup, hands-on local support, dental-tuned default templates.
Scenario 2

Multi-concept group: 8 dental locations + 4 medspa locations under the same operator.

→ TrueLark. The cross-vertical consolidation is the whole pitch.
Scenario 3

40-location DSO on Curve Dental, planning a 12-month phased rollout, wants a formal CSM and quarterly business reviews.

→ TrueLark. Their DSO motion is more developed and Curve coverage is broader. Aria's Curve integration is on the roadmap.
