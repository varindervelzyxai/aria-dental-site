For office managers

Dental Google Business Profile checklist.

A practical pass for the person who runs the front office. Block an afternoon, open the profile on a computer the practice controls, and work the list. The listing is how many new patients choose a dentist. The call that follows still has to reach someone.

The afternoon

Eight jobs, in the order that keeps you from editing a profile you do not control.

Start with access. Then the name, the hours, and the phone. Photos and reviews matter, and they are wasted if the listing sends people to a line nobody answers. This is written for practice owners and office managers, not for patients.

1 · Ownership and access

Know who owns the profile before you change a single field.

Sit down with the profile open and write two things on paper the office keeps: the name of the owner, and the email address on that login. If you cannot name the owner, that is the whole task for today. Categories and photos can wait.

Add a second manager who still works at the practice — the owner-dentist or the office manager. One login should not be the only way in. People leave, vendors get replaced, and a personal inbox gets closed. Approve the second manager while the current owner is in the office and can accept the prompt.

If a former marketing vendor, web designer, or former employee is the only owner, move ownership to the practice before you edit anything else. A profile you cannot log into is a profile you cannot fix when the hours are wrong. Use a practice email the office still controls, not a personal account that walks out with one person. The menus move; the current steps are in Google's Business Profile help.

2 · Name, categories, services

The name on the profile is the name on the door.

Read the business name out loud next to the sign and next to the name on your claims. They should match. Google's guidelines prohibit keyword stuffing in the name. Leave out "best," a service, and "near me." If a previous vendor added words to chase a search, put the real practice name back. For the current rule, use Google's Business Profile help rather than a copied summary.

Pick a primary category that describes what the practice is. A general practice uses Dentist. An orthodontic practice uses Orthodontist. A children's practice uses Pediatric dentist. Extra categories belong only when they are true. A general dentist who sees some children is still a dentist.

Services are the list a caller will mention. Add what you actually offer and can book: exams and cleanings if hygiene is on the schedule, crowns if you do them, clear aligners if you offer them, implants if you place or restore them. Remove anything you stopped doing. The person at the front desk has to recognize the service the patient saw on the listing.

3 · Hours

If the listing says open, someone has to be able to answer.

Set regular hours to the hours the door is open. Then look at lunch. If the desk closes or the office locks the door midday, put that closure on the profile. A listing that stays "open" through lunch is a common reason patients call and reach nobody. The same gap is why lunch-hour coverage is its own problem, separate from the hours field.

Add holiday hours and other special hours before the closure, not the morning you reopen. The day before a long weekend is when people check whether you are open. When hours change, update the profile the same day you update the website and the message callers hear. Three versions of Thursday produce calls the desk is not staffed to take.

4 · Phone and links

The number on the listing is the line that actually gets answered.

Put the main desk line on the profile — the one the team picks up. An old direct line, a fax line, or a number that lived in one person's pocket will ring where nobody is sitting. From a mobile phone that is not the office line, call the listing while you are open, during the lunch closure, and after you lock the door. Write down what the caller hears each time: a person, a ring nobody takes, or a recording. If the open-hours test does not reach a person, fix the line or the coverage before you spend the afternoon on photos. Missed calls from that gap are the subject of missed-call coverage.

The website link should open a working page for this location. Tap it on a phone. You want the page that names this office, shows these hours, and offers a way to book. A parked domain, an error page, or a group homepage that never says which office they reached is a broken link even when it loads. If you set an appointment link, it should open a booking page for this location, not a login wall or a form nobody reads until later in the week.

Add UTM parameters to the website link so profile traffic is visible in analytics. UTM parameters are labels on the end of a URL that tell analytics which link the visit came from. Use your real site address, then append ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp. In analytics, look for the campaign gbp. The labels do not change the page the patient sees. They stop you from guessing whether the profile sends anyone.

5 · Photos

A first-time patient has to recognize the door.

Photograph this office. Take the exterior and the entrance the way someone sees them from the parking row: the sign, the door, and whatever makes the building obvious. Take the reception area they will walk into. Take the team that will greet them, and ask each person before you put their face on the profile. Operatories help when the room looks like the room they will sit in.

Use photographs taken at the practice. A stock smile shows a different door. Refresh the set when something real changes — a remodel, a new sign, a new provider, a front-desk team that no longer matches the picture. If nothing changed, a yearly look through the camera roll is enough. Remove photos of a lobby you no longer have.

6 · Reviews

Ask every patient the same way, and keep the reply general.

Write one sentence and put it where checkout can see it. Use it after every visit, whether the appointment was smooth or not. A sentence you can adapt: "If you are willing, a Google review helps other people find the office. We send the same link to everyone." The same words, the same timing.

Handing the link only to people who say the visit went well is review gating. A discount, a gift, or a drawing entry in exchange for a review is an incentive. Both go against Google's policies. Read the current rules in Google's Business Profile help. Aria's marketing service includes review requests after the visit, so the ask is not a sticky note between check-outs.

Reply to reviews. Thank the person in general terms. Do not confirm that the reviewer is a patient. Do not mention the tooth, the procedure, the insurance, or anything else about care. A shape that stays general: "Thank you for taking the time to write this. The office is here if you want to talk further." That is a practical habit for a dental office. It is not legal advice. If a review needs a real conversation about care, have that conversation at the office, off the public reply. Use the same restraint on a critical review: short, calm, and an invitation to continue at the desk.

7 · Posts and updates

Post when something at the practice actually changed.

A useful post is a fact the front desk will also say out loud. New hours. A provider who joined, and which days they see patients. An insurance plan you now accept, with the desk ready to confirm it on the call. A holiday closure, posted before the day off. A photo of the real entrance after the sign changed.

Keep the pace modest. One true update when something changes is enough. Skip the filler caption when nothing happened that week. Leave ranking out of the reason you post. This page does not promise that posts, photos, or review volume will move the listing.

8 · What happens after the click

The profile's work ends when the phone rings.

Someone taps Call. That call lands on your front desk, on the line you listed above. If nobody answers during lunch, after hours, or while the desk is with a patient at the window, the listing did its job and the office missed the visit. The caller moves to the next practice on the map.

Cover those hours on purpose. Lunch is when the desk is thinnest and new patients still call. After-hours calls arrive when the building is locked. Missed calls are the ones that ring while someone is being checked in. The wider pass for the desk itself is the front desk checklist.

Questions from the desk

Short answers while you still have the profile open.

Who should own the Google Business Profile?

Someone who still works at the practice, using an email the office controls. Add a second manager so one login is not the only way in. If a former vendor or former employee is the only owner, move ownership to the practice before you change anything else.

Can we add keywords to the practice name?

Use the real practice name, the one on the door. Google's guidelines prohibit keyword stuffing in the business name. Current guidelines are in Google's Business Profile help at support.google.com/business.

What should we do when the hours on the profile are wrong?

Set regular hours to match the door, including the lunch closure. Add holiday and special hours before the day off. Update the profile the same day the office hours change. Mismatched hours are a common reason patients call when nobody can answer.

How should we reply to a review?

Thank the person in general terms. Do not confirm that the reviewer is a patient, and do not mention treatment or other details of care. That is a practical habit for a dental office. It is not legal advice.

Do Google posts improve our ranking?

Use posts for occasional updates about real things, such as new hours, a new provider, or insurance you accept. Keep them modest. This page does not promise ranking gains from posts.
