Persona 1 of 5

Solo Practice Owner

Single doctor, 1-3 chairs, often answers their own phones in the evening.

A typical day

Tuesday morning. You have hygiene at 8, comp exam at 9, your one front-desk team member is checking someone in, and the phone is ringing. You let it go to voicemail because you have to. Wednesday evening at 7:42 PM, you're at home and your personal cell is forwarded — a new patient is calling about a toothache, and you're trying to verify their insurance from your couch.

Top 3 pains

  • Every missed call feels personal because the practice is mostly you.
  • Hiring a second front-desk seat costs more than the calls are bringing in.
  • After-hours and weekends are dead zones — and that's when new patients call.

What success looks like

Your phone rings, your patients get answered, and you don't have to be the answer. New patients book themselves into the right slot. After-hours doesn't mean lost. You spend evenings with your family, not on your forwarded line.

How Aria specifically helps

Aria handles overflow, after-hours, and the calls your front desk can't get to. Live insurance verification happens during the call, not after. Most solo practices are live in 2-3 days and see a measurable lift in new-patient bookings within the first month. See how Aria works for solo practices →

Persona 2 of 5

Multi-Doctor Office Manager

Runs a 4-10 chair office, manages 2-4 front-desk staff.

A typical day

You walk in at 7:30, do the morning huddle, then split your day between scheduling, verification, billing escalations, hiring (always hiring), and putting out fires. Two of your front-desk seats are veterans; one is two months in and you're correcting their work. The doctors think the phones run themselves. You know they don't.

Top 3 pains

  • Front-desk turnover is consuming your time — onboarding, retraining, recruiting.
  • Your team is overwhelmed during peak hours and underutilized at others.
  • Insurance verification eats the morning; by the afternoon you're behind on collections.

What success looks like

Your team is calmer because the bell isn't ringing while they're checking patients in. New hires ramp faster because they're not drowning. Verification gets done before the patient arrives, not after. You spend less time recruiting and more time running the operation.

How Aria specifically helps

Aria absorbs phone overflow so your front-desk team can focus on in-person patients. The verification work that used to take 8 hours a week happens in real-time during the booking call. New-hire ramp drops from 90 days to 45 because the workload is sustainable. How Aria reduces front-desk burnout →

Persona 3 of 5

DSO Operations Lead

Covers 5-50 locations, focused on standardization and KPIs.

A typical day

You're on calls all day — regional managers, IT, the new-location integration team, operations review. Your dashboard tracks call answer rate, verification time, conversion rate, no-show rate by location. You see the variance between your top-quartile and bottom-quartile offices and you know it's the front desk, not the doctors. But you can't hire 200 better receptionists.

Top 3 pains

  • Phone-handling variance across locations is your biggest unaddressed quality problem.
  • New-location integrations get bottlenecked on hiring and training front-desk staff.
  • Reporting is downstream of inconsistent data entry across 30 different front desks.

What success looks like

Every location answers the phone the same way. New locations are live in days, not quarters. Your KPI variance compresses because the system, not the staff, is now the constant. You can deploy a process change once and have it in place across all locations the same week.

How Aria specifically helps

Aria deploys centrally across locations with a single configuration surface. Phone answering, verification, and booking become standardized — your bottom-quartile offices perform like your top-quartile ones because the front office is no longer the variable. How Aria scales across DSO locations →

Persona 4 of 5

Specialty Practice Owner

Perio, ortho, or endo. Case-driven, treatment plans matter, longer call cycles.

A typical day

Your practice is referral-heavy. The general dentist sends a patient your way, and that patient calls — usually with questions about cost, about insurance, about whether the treatment will hurt. The conversation is longer than a general-dentistry intake call. Your front desk is good at clinical questions but slow at insurance verification. You miss referral calls during procedures.

Top 3 pains

  • Each missed referral call is high-value lost revenue, not just a $850 first-year visit.
  • Patients want clinical context during the call, not a callback an hour later.
  • Verification is more complex (specialty plans, prior authorization), so it's slower.

What success looks like

Every referral call is answered, and the patient gets enough context to commit. Verification happens before the patient hangs up. Treatment plans are pre-discussed in the booking call so the consultation is more efficient. Your case acceptance rate improves because patients arrive with realistic expectations.

How Aria specifically helps

Aria handles specialty intake conversations with the same depth as a long-tenured front desk. Real-time verification covers specialty riders and prior auth requirements. Clinically nuanced questions escalate to your team without the patient feeling bounced. Specialty-friendly verification →

Persona 5 of 5

New Practice / Startup Dentist

First 18 months. Every patient counts, can't justify a full FTE.

A typical day

You opened six months ago. You hired one front-desk seat. You answer the phone yourself when she's at lunch. You're spending more on marketing than you'd planned because you have to fill the schedule. Every missed call feels like a marketing dollar wasted. You track every patient back to the source and you wince at the cost-per-acquisition number.

Top 3 pains

  • Marketing budget is tight; missed calls feel like burning money.
  • Can't justify hiring a second front-desk seat at current volume.
  • After-hours coverage is non-existent, and that's when a lot of new-patient searches happen.

What success looks like

You capture every patient your marketing brings in. After-hours new-patient searches turn into Tuesday-morning bookings. You're hitting 40% conversion on inbound calls instead of 25%. Cost-per-acquired-patient drops, which lets you reinvest in the channels that are working.

How Aria specifically helps

Aria gives you 24/7 coverage at a fraction of a second front-desk seat's cost. Every marketing-driven call gets answered.
