How Much Do Missed Calls Cost Your Dental Practice? A Transparent Breakdown

Missed calls are visits the practice never gets to book. What they are worth is your own case mix. We do not publish a dollar loss per practice.

Search for missed-call articles and you will find large annual dollar figures with vague math, each one selling a product. Aria does not publish a dollar loss per practice.

We're going to do something different. We'll walk through the actual calculation — step by step, with sources labeled — so you can plug in your own numbers and judge for yourself. Most of the underlying data comes from industry reports and vendor call-tracking studies. We'll clearly distinguish what is peer-reviewed versus what is vendor-reported.

What you can know without a published miss rate

Vendor call-tracking companies publish miss rates. Those figures come from companies that sell a solution, and this page does not repeat them as facts. What a practice can see is operational: if one person is on the phone, at the glass, or at lunch, the next ring goes to voicemail. Callers who reach voicemail often try the next practice.

What a missed call is worth depends on that practice's case mix, show rate, and whether the caller was a new patient. Use your own call log and the ROI calculator. We do not publish a dollar loss per practice.

The limits around a single front desk

Missed calls sit on top of staffing limits. When a receptionist leaves, the phones are thin during the transition. A second hire is a full salary plus benefits, and it still leaves nights, lunch, and the second simultaneous ring uncovered.

Opportunity cost. When your receptionist is on a call, every other incoming call goes to voicemail. When she's at lunch, calls go to voicemail. When she's checking in a patient, the phone rings unanswered. These are structural limits of one person covering several jobs at once.

Three ways practices handle this today

Option 1: Hire another receptionist

Adds a second person for phone coverage during peak hours and lunch. It does not cover the building when it is closed, and each location needs its own hire.

Option 2: Dental answering service

Live operators take messages and relay information after hours. They typically cannot access your calendar, verify insurance, or book into the live schedule.

Option 3: AI receptionist

Aria answers inbound calls 24/7. It can use the schedule the practice connects, check eligibility, book appointments, and collect payment when those are turned on. It works across voice, chat, and SMS. It does not call patients.

A second receptionist can handle complex in-person situations and still cannot cover every simultaneous ring. An answering service covers after-hours message-taking and usually cannot book into the live schedule. Aria covers inbound volume and still hands clinical or always-human asks to a person. For a deeper comparison, see the comparison page.

When inbound AI answering makes sense

Good fit: the desk is already missing rings, after-hours calls go to voicemail, you run more than one location, or the front desk is overwhelmed.

Less clear fit: the desk reliably answers the volume you have, and the conversations are highly specialized. After-hours inbound coverage can still help.
