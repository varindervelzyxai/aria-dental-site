What Should a Dental Practice Ask Before Hiring a Marketing Agency?

Direct answer

Ask who owns the website, domain, Google Business Profile, and ad accounts. Ask how results are measured: calls and booked appointments, not only rankings or traffic. Ask whether they follow Google's guidelines and never promise a number-one ranking. Ask what happens to the calls the work produces, and how you leave.

Marketing Last updated November 18, 2026 7 min read By Aria Dental

This page is for a practice owner or office manager comparing agencies for the website, SEO, the Google Business Profile, ads, and social.

Who will own the website, domain, Google Business Profile, and ad accounts?

The practice should own them. The agency gets manager access on its own login, so you can remove it.

That means the domain, the website files, the Google Business Profile, and the ad accounts. On the profile, the practice's Google Account should be the primary owner, and the agency a manager. Google's page on owners and managers says a profile can have more than one owner, and only one primary owner. Managers can edit info, respond to reviews, and manage posts. They cannot add or remove users or remove the profile. Each person needs their own Google Account, so nobody shares a password.

Ask the same for Google Ads and any other ad account. If the agency built the profile or the campaigns on its own login, move primary ownership to the practice before you sign. The Google Business Profile checklist shows who is on the listing now.

How will you measure results?

Count calls and booked appointments, including new-patient calls by source. Rankings and traffic are not a booked visit.

Ask for calls, booked appointments, and the source of each one: the landing page, the campaign, or the listing. Tie call tracking to those pages. How a dental practice tracks marketing is the walkthrough. Ask which calls were new patients, and which became a visit on the schedule. A report that stops at rankings and sessions does not show whether a chair was filled.

Do you follow Google's guidelines, and what do you promise?

They should follow Google's guidelines, and they should not promise first place. Google does not sell organic rankings, and ads do not change them.

Google's page on hiring an SEO is the interview. Ask for previous work, whether they follow the Google Search Essentials, what results to expect and in what timeframe, how they measure success, their experience in dentistry and in your city, and whether they will share every change they make to the site and why. If they guarantee first place, find someone else. No one can guarantee a number-one ranking. For an audit, grant read access to Search Console only, not write access. The audit should estimate the work. It should not promise a ranking.

Google never accepts money to include or rank a site in organic results, and advertising does not affect those results. Ads can place you in the ad slots. They do not buy the unpaid listing.

Ask about pages and links. Google's spam policies describe doorway abuse, such as city pages that funnel people to one destination, and link spam, such as buying or exchanging links to manipulate rankings. You want pages a patient can use. Google says violating those policies may hurt the site's presence in results, and that you are responsible for what a company you hire does to the site.

What exactly is included each month?

Get a written list of the work. "Full service" is not a list.

Name the website updates, including a separate page for each service patients search and book. Name who posts on the Google Business Profile, who adds photos, and who replies to reviews. Name whether ads are included, and that the practice owns the account. Name who creates the Instagram and TikTok posts and who publishes them. Put that in the scope you sign. If the site, the listing, and social are three vendors, see that before you compare retainers.

Who answers the phone when the marketing works?

Ask who picks up at lunch, after hours, and when the desk is with a patient. Ranking and ads produce calls, and a missed call is not a booked visit.

Ask what the caller hears, what can be booked, and what is written down for the team. Nights and weekends are the same question: after-hours answering. Aria answers the inbound lines the practice assigns, after hours, at lunch, and during busy moments, so patients can book when the desk cannot pick up. Aria answers inbound calls only.

How do we leave, and what do we keep?

Ask the term, the notice, and what you keep, and get that in the agreement before you sign.

Ask how long it runs and how either side ends it. The domain, the website files, primary ownership of the Google Business Profile, and the ad accounts should stay with the practice, including the day you leave. Ask for the content and the access you will need once the agency is off the account. Agreements differ, and there is no single length to assume. Read the sentences on term, notice, and ownership.

What does a straight answer sound like?

A clear answer names an owner, a report, or a line in the agreement.

Frequently asked questions

Should a dental practice own its Google Business Profile?

Yes. Google's help page says a profile can have more than one owner and only one primary owner. The practice should be that primary owner. The agency should be a manager on its own Google Account. Managers can edit info, respond to reviews, and manage posts, and they cannot add or remove users or remove the profile. Do not share a password.

Can an agency guarantee first place on Google?

No. Google's page on hiring an SEO says that if someone guarantees first place, find someone else. No one can guarantee a number-one ranking. Google never accepts money to rank sites in organic results, and advertising does not affect those results.

How long should a dental marketing contract be?

Ask for the term, the notice, and what you keep when it ends, and get that in the written agreement. Practices use different terms. There is no single length to copy.

Should my marketing agency also handle my phones?

Someone has to. Ask who answers at lunch, after hours, and when the desk is with a patient. Ranking and ads create calls, and a missed call is not a booked visit. The desk can keep the phones. Aria answers inbound calls only.

What access should I give an SEO for an audit?
