What Should a Dental Office Voicemail Greeting Say?

Name the practice, say whether the office is closed or the line is only busy, state the hours and when someone will call back, say what to leave, point to online booking if you have it, and give a non-clinical emergency line. Keep the recording short enough to finish in one breath.

This page is for the owner or office manager rewriting the recording callers hear when nobody picks up: lunch, after hours, weekends, holidays, and a line that is already busy.

What should a dental office voicemail greeting include?

Six lines cover it: who you are, whether anyone can pick up, when a person will call back, what to leave, where to go online, and one emergency line.

"We are unavailable and will call back as soon as possible" skips the practice name, the situation, and a window the desk can keep. The caller cannot tell lunch from a holiday.

Line What the caller should hear
Practice name The name on the door and the website. No street address.
Situation Closed, lunch until [time], or lines busy while the office is open.
Hours and callback [hours], and the window when a person will return the message.
What to leave Name, callback number, and a short reason.
Online path [website], and only if the practice takes visit requests there.
Emergency Medical emergency: hang up and call 911. After hours: the practice's own instructions on [website].

Say the true situation, and state a callback window only if the desk can keep it. The emergency line is one sentence: "If this is a medical emergency, hang up and call 911." When the office is closed, point to the after-hours instructions the practice already posts. Do not name a condition or say what a symptom means.

What should a dental voicemail greeting leave out?

Ask for a name, a callback number, and a short reason. Leave health details and a long menu off the recording.

A short reason is "cleaning" or "question about hours." Leave symptoms, medication names, insurance ID numbers, and a date of birth off the prompt. The desk hears that recording later. The person who returns the call can take those details.

Stop after those six lines. A provider list, a service list, parking rules, or a joke gives the caller time to hang up before the beep. Leave out a second phone number and any promise of a clinical answer on the callback.

Should a dental office use different greetings for lunch, after hours, and holidays?

Yes. The caller needs to know which situation they reached, because the callback promise is different.

Lunch. The office is open. Say when the desk is back, and that lunch messages are returned that same day, during [hours]. That midday gap is covered in lunch-hour front desk coverage. A closed-office greeting at lunch tells callers you are shut when the lights are on.

After hours. The building is closed. State [hours], and say messages are returned on the next business day, during [callback window]. If the desk starts in the morning, say the morning.

Weekends and holidays. Name the closure and the reopen day. For a normal weekend, put the closed day, such as Saturday, in place of [holiday name]. Weekend dental call coverage is the operating side. The greeting is only the recording.

Busy line. If the greeting plays because the lines are taken, say the office is open and every line is with a patient. That is a different fact from "we are closed." Promise a callback inside [callback window] only when the desk can keep it.

Record each version on its own, and switch it at lunch, at close, and the afternoon before a holiday.

What should a dental office voicemail script say?

Use the version that matches the situation, with [Practice Name], [hours], and the other brackets filled in. Drop the website line if you do not take visit requests online.

Read it aloud once, and cut any line you would not say at the desk.

Lunch. "You have reached [Practice Name]. The office is open today, and the team is at lunch until [time]. Please leave your name, a callback number, and a short reason for your call. We will return calls when the desk is back, during [hours]. You can also request a visit at [website]. If this is a medical emergency, hang up and call 911."

After hours. "You have reached [Practice Name]. The office is closed. Our hours are [hours]. Please leave your name, a callback number, and a short reason for your call. We return messages on the next business day, during [callback window]. You can request a visit at [website]. If this is a medical emergency, hang up and call 911. For a dental concern after hours, follow the instructions on [website]."

Holiday or weekend closure. "You have reached [Practice Name]. The office is closed for [holiday name] and reopens [reopen day], during [hours]. Please leave your name, a callback number, and a short reason for your call. We will return calls after we reopen. If this is a medical emergency, hang up and call 911. After-hours instructions are on [website]."

Lines busy, office open. "You have reached [Practice Name]. The office is open, and every line is with a patient. Please leave your name, a callback number, and a short reason for your call. We will call you back during [hours], inside [callback window]. You can also request a visit at [website]. If this is a medical emergency, hang up and call 911."

Replace every bracket, then call the line from a cell phone. You should hear the practice name, the situation, the callback window, and the emergency line before the beep.

How fast should a dental office return voicemails?

Return each message inside the window the greeting already stated. This site does not publish a standard return time.

The recording does not return the call. A person does. "As soon as possible" is not a window. "When the desk is back from lunch" is a window. "The next business day, during [callback window]" is a window.

  1. Write that window in the greeting.
  2. Name one person who clears the box when the window starts.
  3. Call back with the name and the short reason. If it needs a clinical answer, hand it to the person the practice assigns.
  4. If messages are still in the box at the next huddle, the window is too long or nobody owns it. Change the greeting or the owner.

A lunch message returned the next morning breaks a same-day promise.

Where does a dental voicemail greeting fall short?

A greeting only helps the caller who stays on the line and leaves a message. Many callers who reach voicemail hang up without leaving a name, a number, or a reason, and try another practice.

Frequently asked questions

What should callers leave on a dental office voicemail?

A name, a callback number, and a short reason for the call, such as a cleaning or a question about hours. Do not ask for symptoms, medications, insurance numbers, or a date of birth. The person who calls back can take those details.

Should lunch, after-hours, and holiday greetings be the same recording?

No. A lunch greeting says the office is open and names when the desk is back. An after-hours greeting says the office is closed and names the next callback window. A holiday greeting names the holiday and the day the office reopens.

How fast should a dental office return voicemails?
