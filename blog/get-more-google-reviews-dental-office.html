How Can a Dental Office Get More Google Reviews Without Breaking the Rules?

Direct answer

Ask every patient the same way, at checkout, with the review link from your Google Business Profile. Do not ask only people who liked the visit, and do not offer a discount, gift, or payment. Do not write the review for them. Leave treatment out of the message and out of any public reply.

Reviews Last updated: November 11, 2026 7 min read By Aria Dental

This page is for a practice owner or office manager. It is a habit, not legal advice. Check a public reply with your privacy officer or counsel before you post it.

When should a dental office ask for a Google review?

Ask at checkout, the same day, while the patient is still in the office. If they have left, send the same link that day.

Google's help on creating a review link says to put that link on receipts, in thank-you emails, at the end of a chat, or on a QR code in the office. The QR code is made in a computer browser, not on a phone.

Do not wait to learn whether they liked the visit, and do not make the review a condition of leaving. Google's Maps content policy says not to require or pressure a review while the person is on the premises, and not to request specific wording.

How should the front desk ask?

Use one short script and the link from the Google Business Profile. Leave the star and the sentences to the patient.

  1. Open the Business Profile. Select Read reviews, then Get more reviews, and copy the link. That path is on Google's review-link page.
  2. At checkout, say: "If you have a minute, a Google review helps other people decide. Here is the link. Whatever you write is fine."
  3. If they want it in writing, send that same link. Google's help names email, a WhatsApp message, and a Facebook post. A line on the receipt works too.
  4. Stop. Do not suggest a star, a sentence, a staff name, or a quota for the desk. Google's policy lists those asks.

What is allowed, and what is not?

The same ask for every patient is allowed. A filter, a payment, a scripted review, or a review from the staff is not.

What the office does Allowed?
Ask every patient, same link, no reward Yes. Genuine visit, no reward, no suggested rating or wording.
Ask only patients who liked the visit No. Google bars selective positive asks.
Discount, gift, or payment for a review No on Google. The FTC rule also bars a reward for a positive or negative review.
Write the review, or hand over a paragraph to paste No. Their words, not the office's.
Owner, staff, or family reviews the office No. Google removes conflict-of-interest posts, including staff and family.
Buy a review from someone who was not there No. Fake engagement on Google, and a banned purchase under the FTC rule.

Is review gating allowed?

No. Asking only patients who say the visit went well, or sending unhappy patients to a private form and happy patients to Google, is review gating. Google does not allow it.

The Maps policy says a business may not "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." A tablet that shows the Google link only after a top score does that, and so does sending unhappy patients to the manager instead. Google's tips say a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy.

The FTC published its Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials in the Federal Register on August 22, 2024. It took effect on October 21, 2024. The text is also in the FTC's copy of the final rule. The Commission says the section on buying positive or negative reviews does not cover review gating, and that review gating can still violate section 5 of the FTC Act.

Can a dentist offer a discount or a gift for a review?

No. A discount, a gift, or a payment for a Google review is an incentive, and Google prohibits it.

The Maps policy names payment, discounts, and free goods or services for posting a review, changing one, or removing a negative one. A gift card or a raffle entry fits that list. Google's tips page states the same ban, and those reviews are removed.

Google might also restrict the profile. Its restrictions page lists a period with no new reviews, existing reviews unpublished for a period, or a warning. The owner is emailed and can appeal. The FTC rule bars a reward tied to a positive or negative sentiment, such as a gift only for five stars. Google also bars a reward for any rating. A review the office writes and posts under the patient's name is not that person's review. The FTC rule bars creating or buying it when the business knew or should have known.

What should the request and the public reply leave out?

Leave the treatment out of the request. In a public reply, do not confirm that the person is a patient, and do not add treatment, insurance, or cost.

Use: "Thanks for visiting [practice name]. Here is the link if you want to leave a Google review." Leave out the procedure, the tooth, the diagnosis, and the fee.

OCR's page on New Vision Dental, a California practice, says review replies disclosed patients' protected health information and calls that illegal under HIPAA. The practice paid $23,000 and agreed to a corrective action plan. The resolution agreement says the Yelp replies included names plus visit and insurance details, that the investigation indicated an impermissible disclosure, and that it is not an admission of liability.

OCR's page on Dr. U. Phillip Igbinadolor, D.M.D. & Associates, a North Carolina practice, says a reply to a negative online review disclosed a patient's protected health information. OCR imposed a $50,000 civil money penalty. Wording that stays off the chart is in the reply post.

How should the office reply once the review is up?

Reply in a few sentences. Do not confirm the reviewer is a patient, and do not mention treatment, insurance, or cost. Google's tips say to reply and to keep private information out of the reply. The reply post has wording that thanks the person and moves the details off the public page.

How do unanswered calls show up in Google reviews?

A review about the phone is still a review. If nobody answers, it can describe the hold or the voicemail.

The reply post notes that many complaints are about not reaching the office. Why dental practices miss calls says the next ring often goes to voicemail while someone is being checked in. This site does not publish a miss rate. List the number that rings that office. If it goes to voicemail at lunch or after close, the call button on the listing fails.

Frequently asked questions

When should a dental office ask for a Google review?

Ask at checkout on the day of the visit, with the same Google link for every patient. If they have already left, send that link the same day.

Is review gating allowed for a dental office?

No. Google bars asking only patients who liked the visit. The FTC's 2024 rule does not put review gating in its incentive section, and says review gating can still violate section 5 of the FTC Act.

Can a dental office offer a discount or a gift for a Google review?

No. Google prohibits payment, discounts, and free goods or services in exchange for posting a review, changing a review, or removing a negative review. The FTC rule prohibits incentives conditioned on a review being positive or negative.

Can the dentist or the staff review their own practice on Google?

No. Google removes reviews from current or former staff and from family, because those ties are a conflict of interest. An officer or manager who writes one must also disclose that relationship under the FTC rule.

What should a dental review request leave out?

Leave out the procedure, the tooth, the diagnosis, and the fee. A usable message names the practice and includes the Google review link. In a public reply, do not confirm that the reviewer is a patient or mention treatment.

Does Aria call patients to ask for a Google review?

No. Aria answers inbound calls on the lines the practice assigns. The marketing page says Aria can send the review request after the visit, under the same rules as the front desk.
