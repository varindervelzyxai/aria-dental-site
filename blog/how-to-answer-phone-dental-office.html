How Should a Dental Office Answer the Phone? A Greeting Script That Works

Direct answer

Answer with the practice name, your first name, and a short offer to help. Ask before you put someone on hold, and say when you will be back. Take the name and callback number early. Transfer with that context. End by repeating the next step. Use the same greeting for overflow and after-hours calls.

Front desk Last updated: November 20, 2026 6 min read By Aria Dental

This page is for a practice owner or office manager who wants one live greeting the whole team can say. It covers the answer, a hold, a second ringing line, a name and callback number, a transfer, and the close. When nobody picks up, use the voicemail greeting instead of this script.

What should a dental office say when it answers the phone?

Say the practice name, your first name, and a short offer to help. Then stop and let the caller speak.

"Thank you for calling [Practice Name]. This is [First Name]. How can I help you?" is the whole greeting. Use the name on the door and the website. Your first name shows a person has the line. Keep it to one breath. Leave out the street, the providers, and the service list.

Say the same three parts on every call. Questions for a first visit are on what to ask a new patient on the phone. Do not recite them before you know why they called.

What should you say before you put a caller on hold?

Ask first, name a short time you can keep, and come back when you said you would.

"May I place you on a brief hold? I will be back in about a minute." If they say yes, return inside that time and say you are back. If they say no, finish the step you are on, or take a name and number and say who will call back.

"Please hold," with no question and no time, is how callers leave. If you need longer than you named, come back and offer a callback.

How should the desk handle a second ringing line?

Stay with the caller you already have. Do not grab the new line without a word.

Ask if you may hold, and name the time. If they agree, answer the new line with the same greeting, take a name and callback number, and return. If they do not agree, stay with them. Who covers that ring is on who answers the second line during check-in. Do not answer the new caller with "Dental office, hold on."

When should you ask for the caller's name and callback number?

Ask early, before you hold, transfer, or look something up, in case the call drops.

"May I have your name and the best number to reach you, in case we get disconnected?" Spell the name back. Read the number back. Write both down before you leave the line, even when you expect to finish. A note that says only "call back" cannot be returned.

If someone calls for another person, write both names and who the visit is for. Keep the reason in their words. Do not turn it into a diagnosis, and do not take a medical history on this call.

How do you transfer a call without a cold handoff?

Tell the caller who is next and why, stay until that person is there, and pass the name, the number, and the reason before you leave.

A cold handoff is "please hold" and a click. The caller starts over. Say you will connect them with [Name or role] and stay until that person picks up. Then one sentence: name, number, and the reason in their words. Leave only after they confirm they have it.

If nobody is free, do not drop the caller into silence. Name who will call back and a window the desk can keep.

How should you end a dental office call?

End on a next step the caller can repeat: a day and time, or who will call, when, and at which number.

If the schedule has the visit, say the name, the day, and the time, and ask, "Did I get that right?" Do not offer a time that is not open. If you cannot finish, "[Name or role] will call you back [window], at the number you gave me" is a next step. "Someone will call you" is not. Then stop.

Should overflow and after-hours calls use the same greeting?

Yes. A teammate at lunch, a person covering a busy moment, or an AI receptionist on the inbound line should use the same three parts.

Post one line at the desk: the practice name, the name of whoever is answering, and an offer to help. A caller in the evening should hear the practice name.

What does a short dental office phone script sound like?

Use these lines. Cut any line you would not say at the desk.

Answer. "Thank you for calling [Practice Name]. This is [First Name]. How can I help you?"

Name and number. "May I have your name and the best number to reach you, in case we get disconnected?" Spell the name. Read the number back.

Hold. "May I place you on a brief hold? I will be back in about a minute." Then: "[Caller name], thank you for holding. I am back."

Second line. "Another line is ringing. May I place you on a brief hold?" Answer it with the same greeting, then return.

Transfer. "I am going to connect you with [Name or role]. I will stay on until they pick up and tell them why you called."

Close. "You are set for [day] at [time]. Did I get that right?" If you did not book: "[Name or role] will call you back [window], at the number you gave me."

A question about pain, medication, or care after a procedure goes to the practice in the caller's words. Do not answer it from this script.

What should the person answering do, and what should they avoid?

Do the short version. Avoid the line that makes the caller start over or hang up.

Do Avoid
Answer with the practice name, your first name, and "How can I help you?" "Dental office" with no name, or a greeting that lists services before the caller can speak.
Ask before a hold, name a short time, and come back. "Please hold," then silence.
Take the name and callback number before you leave the line, and read both back. Waiting until the end, or a note that says only "call back."
Say who you are transferring to, stay on the line, and pass the name, number, and reason. A cold transfer that makes the caller start the story over.
Close by repeating the day and time, or who will call, when, and at which number. "Someone will call you," with no person and no window.
Use that same greeting for overflow and after hours, including an AI receptionist. A different opener at night, or an answer with no practice name.

Frequently asked questions

What should a dental office say when it answers the phone?

Say the practice name, your first name, and a short offer to help. "Thank you for calling [Practice Name]. This is [First Name]. How can I help you?" is enough. Skip a long welcome, a menu, and a service list.

What should you say before you put a dental caller on hold?

Ask first, name a short time you can keep, and come back when you said you would. If the caller says no, finish the step you are on or take a name and callback number. Do not say please hold and leave the line.

When should a dental office ask for a callback number?

Ask for the name and callback number early, before you hold, transfer, or look something up. Spell the name back and read the number back. If the call drops, the desk has a way to return it.

How do you transfer a dental call without a cold handoff?

Tell the caller who you are connecting and why, and stay on the line until that person is there. Pass the caller's name, callback number, and reason, and leave only after they have it. If nobody is free, name who will call back and the window.

Should overflow and after-hours calls use the same dental greeting?

Yes. A teammate at lunch, a person covering a busy line, and an AI receptionist on the inbound line should use the same greeting.

What should you say at the end of a dental office call?

Repeat the next step and wait for a yes. That is the day and time if a visit was booked, or who will call back, the window, and the number if it was not. Someone will call you is not a next step.
