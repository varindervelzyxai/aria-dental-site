Should a Dental Office Hire Another Front Desk Person or Use an AI Receptionist?

Direct answer

Hire another front desk person when people in the office are the constraint: check-in lines, checkout, insurance paperwork, and patients standing at the window. Use an AI receptionist when the constraint is inbound calls that ring while the desk is already with someone, including after hours. A second wage does not answer a line nobody is free to pick up.

Staffing Last updated: November 6, 2026 7 min read By Aria Dental

This page is for a practice owner or office manager choosing another full-time person, or inbound coverage for calls that person cannot take. Lunch, the second line, and after-hours have their own pages.

When is hiring another front desk person the right move?

Hire when the waiting work needs a person in the building. Check-in, checkout, forms, a payment at the window, and walking a patient back all require someone standing there.

A second person covers one shift: the greeting, checkout, and the patient in front of them. The office manager page follows that day. Hire when one person cannot be at the window and in the back at once.

That person still has one pair of hands. A call that rings while they are at the window waits unless someone else is free. Paid time off, a sick day, and training weeks stay open unless you staff them. Lunch is a different decision, covered in the lunch-hour post.

When is the bottleneck inbound calls, not chairside capacity?

The bottleneck is the line when calls ring out while the desk is with a patient, or after close, and the schedule still had a visit you would have booked. A person already in the lobby does not pick up that ring.

See the second-line post, after-hours answering, and the missed-calls page for those windows. This page decides whether to hire or cover the line.

On a week the book still had openings, lobby waits mean hire. Dead calls mean the line is the bottleneck. If every visit type callers want is already full, more answers do not create a chair.

What does another front desk hire actually cover that an AI receptionist does not?

A hire covers work in the office. An AI receptionist covers assigned inbound calls, chat, and SMS, and does not stand at the window or take a walk-in payment.

  • Greeting and check-in for a patient in the lobby.
  • Checkout and payments at the desk.
  • Forms, IDs, and cards a person hands over.
  • Walking a patient back and coordinating in the hallway.
  • A call the practice wants a person to finish, including one forwarded while the desk is open.

Recall stays with the people you employ. Aria does not dial that list. A new hire also leaves nights, weekends, and the minutes when both people are with patients uncovered, unless you staff those hours or assign the line.

How do practices combine a human desk with AI overflow?

Keep the person for the lobby and for calls they are free to take. Send the ring that arrives while they are with a patient, and the hours after they leave, to coverage that books an allowed visit or hands the call back.

The desk stays first when someone is free. Aria answers assigned inbound calls, forwards when the desk is open, or leaves a callback with who called, why, and what the caller was told. What questions should you ask before you hire or buy?

Ask whether the miss is a person absent from the lobby or a call that never got an answer. Then ask what coverage may book, where the booking is written, and who takes a clinical question.

  1. If check-in, checkout, payments, forms, or walking patients back wait because one person cannot be in two places, hire.
  2. If calls rang during a patient conversation or after close while the schedule had an opening, the bottleneck is the line.
  3. Leave lunch, the second line, and after-hours out of the headcount decision. One new shift covers one person's hours.
  4. If you hire, name the shift, the paid-time-off cover, and who answers when that person is with a patient.
  5. If you add inbound coverage, list approved visit types and providers, whether eligibility checks are on, and that clinical questions go to the practice.
  6. Confirm write-back: Open Dental is live, Google Calendar sync is available, and Dentrix and Eaglesoft are on the roadmap.
  7. Keep the current phones. The FAQ says no number port is required. Pricing is a custom Solo, Growing, or DSO quote.
  8. Decide what the desk still gets: a forward when it is open, or a callback with who called, why, and what the caller was told.
