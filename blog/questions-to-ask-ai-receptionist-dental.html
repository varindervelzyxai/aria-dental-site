What Should a Dental Office Ask Before Choosing an AI Receptionist?

Direct answer

Ask which inbound calls it answers, when it picks up, how a person gets the call, and where the booking shows on the schedule. Ask how it handles insurance and urgent calls, what you can review afterward, how you leave, and whether you can call it yourself before you sign.

Buying Last updated: November 27, 2026 7 min read By Aria Dental

Take this list into the vendor call. The complete guide covers the product. Hiring is a staffing question. Forwarding is the setup page, and handoffs belong in your transfer rules.

What calls should it answer?

Inbound calls the practice assigns. Recall and other outbound calls stay with the office.

What a good answer sounds like. Inbound only, approved visit types and providers, and recall stays with the practice.

Red flag. A promise to call overdue patients or run outbound recall.

When should it pick up?

When the practice chooses: every inbound call, or only the calls the desk does not answer.

What a good answer sounds like. All calls, no-answer, busy, or a lunch and after-hours schedule, on your current phones, with no number change.

Red flag. Only one mode, a required number change, or unanswered calls that still land in voicemail.

How does a call reach a person?

By a rule the practice wrote. A transfer reaches the desk when it is open, or leaves a callback a person can read.

What a good answer sounds like. A transfer to an open desk, or a callback with who called, why, and what the caller was told.

Red flag. Every hard question goes to voicemail, or the desk gets a transfer with no name and no reason.

Where does the appointment land?

On the schedule the desk already uses, for a visit type the practice allowed. Watch a test booking appear there.

What a good answer sounds like. A test visit appears in the system you named, while you watch, and they say what is live and what is still on the roadmap.

Red flag. A claim of native Dentrix write-back with nothing on the Dentrix schedule, or a morning spreadsheet nobody mentioned.

What should it do with an insurance question?

Check eligibility on the call only when the practice turns that on, and send an unclear result to the team.

What a good answer sounds like. They run a parent calling for a child and say what the check returned. A missing result goes to the practice.

Red flag. A procedure fee quoted without a check, or a claim that every plan is covered when they will not run yours on the demo.

What should happen on an urgent call?

It follows the practice's written rules and routes the call to staff or the on-call person. It does not give clinical advice.

What a good answer sounds like. They read back who is on call, what may be booked, and what must go to a person. Pain, medication, and questions after a procedure go to the practice.

Red flag. The service names the problem, suggests a medication, or tells the caller to wait until Monday with no path to a person.

What will callers hear, and what can you review?

Hear the greeting yourself. After the call, the practice should be able to see what happened.

What a good answer sounds like. The greeting uses the practice name, a caller who asks is told it is an AI, and you can open a summary or recording that says who called, why, and the result.

Red flag. Only a polished recording of someone else's call, no summary, or a recording the practice is not allowed to hear.

How long is setup, and how do you leave?

Ask the day live calls start, the term, and what you keep if you cancel. Read those lines before you sign.

What a good answer sounds like. A kickoff, a go-live window, the term, how you cancel, and an export of calls and bookings.

Red flag. An annual contract before anyone has placed a test call, a fee to get your own call history, or no written way to leave.

How do you test it before you commit?

Call it yourself, on the calls your desk actually gets, before any patient uses it.

What a good answer sounds like. A live demo you can interrupt: a new patient, a reschedule, insurance, an urgent call, and a transfer. Then remove the test visit.

Red flag. A slide deck, a recording you cannot interrupt, or a go-live date before anyone at the practice has called it.

Frequently asked questions

What should a dental office ask before choosing an AI receptionist?

Ask which calls it answers, when it picks up, where the booking lands, how urgent calls and insurance are handled, and whether you can call it before you sign.

Should a dental AI receptionist place outbound recall calls?

No. Ask the vendor to say it answers inbound calls only. Recall and other outbound calls stay with the practice. Aria answers inbound calls. It does not dial an overdue recall list or place outbound recall.

Can an AI receptionist book into Dentrix or Eaglesoft?

Ask to see a test booking land in the schedule you use. Treat Dentrix and Eaglesoft as on the roadmap until you see that booking. Do not accept a claim of native Dentrix write-back without the visit on that schedule.

What should happen when a caller says the visit is urgent?

Follow the practice's written rules. Route the call to staff or the on-call person. The service should not give clinical advice, name a medication, or tell the caller what the problem is.

How do you test a dental AI receptionist before you sign?

Call it yourself. Use a new-patient booking, a reschedule, an insurance question, an urgent call, and a transfer to the desk. Check the schedule and the summary, then remove the test visit. A slide deck is not the test.

How long should setup take, and can you leave?

Ask for the kickoff, the day live calls start, the term, and the export you get if you cancel. Read those sentences in the agreement before you sign.
