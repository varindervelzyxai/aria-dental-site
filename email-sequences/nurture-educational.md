## Email 2 — Day 1: The number that changes the conversation

**Subject:** The 62% that's costing you patients
**Preview:** And how to figure out yours in five minutes.

Hey {{first_name}},

Here's a stat from the guide that gets the most pushback in early calls with practice owners: **about 62% of inbound dental calls go unanswered during peak hours and after-hours combined.**

Owners' first reaction: "no way, our team answers every call." Then we run the audit. It's almost always between 50% and 70%.

Here's the five-minute test to find your number:

1. Pull last week's call log from your phone system or VOIP.
2. Filter to inbound only.
3. Count calls under 15 seconds. Those are voicemails or hang-ups.
4. Divide by total inbound. That's your floor — actual missed-call rate is higher because some "answered" calls are actually 30-second goodbyes.

If you'd rather have us run it for you and walk through what we find, reply with "audit" and I'll send a calendar link.

## Email 3 — Day 2: What "AI receptionist" actually means

**Subject:** A short defense of the word "AI"
**Preview:** Because it's been pretty thoroughly ruined.

Hey {{first_name}},

The phrase "AI receptionist" has been beaten to death. Half the products that use it are decision trees with a synthetic voice. The other half are call-center routers wearing AI in the marketing.

Here's how I'd separate the two on a vendor call. Three questions:

**1. "Can it handle a question I haven't trained it on?"**
A real AI agent will reason through it or escalate gracefully. A scripted bot will say "I'm sorry, I didn't catch that" three times and hang up.

**2. "Can it switch languages mid-call without me clicking anything?"**
Aria, ChatGPT-class agents — yes. IVR trees and old-school voice bots — no.

**3. "Can it actually verify insurance during the live call, not async after?"**
This is the dividing line between practice tools and patient-experience tools. If verification happens after the call, the patient already lost trust.

If a vendor flunks any of these, that's your answer.

— {{rep_first_name}}

## Email 4 — Day 4: The HIPAA section nobody reads

**Subject:** The boring email about HIPAA
**Preview:** Read it once and you can stop worrying.

Hey {{first_name}},

Most owners skip the HIPAA section in vendor docs because it's written by lawyers for lawyers. Here's the practical version, in 60 seconds.

For an AI receptionist to be HIPAA-compliant for a dental practice, three things have to be true:

- **A signed BAA between you and the vendor**, and a signed BAA between the vendor and every subprocessor (their LLM provider, telephony layer, transcription, storage). If they can't show you the chain, that's a no.
- **Encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3).** Standard. If they hedge on this, walk.
- **Role-based access and audit logs.** Every conversation, every recording, who viewed it, when. You should be able to pull a log on demand.

That's it. Anyone selling you a "HIPAA-compliant AI" without those three is selling you exposure.
