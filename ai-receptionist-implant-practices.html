Implant dentistry

AI in implant practices.

Implant cases close in 6–18 months from first inquiry. AI front office is built for that arc — but it has to be implemented thoughtfully.

The implant arc

From inquiry to chair time.

Implant cases have one of the longest sales cycles in dental. A patient who sees a TV ad in January may research in February, call you in March, get a CT scan in April, hear about cost in May, get insurance review in June, and start treatment in July. Most implant practices lose 40–60% of inquiries somewhere along this arc — usually because someone forgets to follow up at the right moment.

AI front office is uniquely good at the long-arc problem. It doesn't forget. It can run a 9-touch nurture sequence over 6 months without missing a beat. And it doesn't get tired of the same anxious questions about pain, healing time, and insurance coverage.

Critically, AI doesn't fake clinical authority. The clinical conversation about whether the patient is a candidate for an implant, what the surgical approach should be, and what the timeline looks like belongs to your implant surgeon. Aria handles the around-it work. See Aria for implant dentistry for the full breakdown.

Referring GP handoffs

The most under-optimized channel in implant practice.

Most implant practices get 50–80% of new cases via GP referral. The referring GP's office calls; your front desk picks up; the patient is supposed to get scheduled. In practice, this hand-off breaks all the time. The receptionist who took the call moves on, the patient never gets a follow-up call, the GP wonders why their patient didn't get treated.

AI receptionists handle the GP-referral hand-off as a structured workflow. The referring doctor's name, scope of referral, patient urgency, and any notes are captured in one place and surfaced to the implant team. The patient gets a follow-up call within 24 hours. The GP's office gets a confirmation that their patient is in the system.

Practices that fix this referral handoff alone often see meaningful case-volume increase — not because of new marketing, but because they stopped losing the cases they were already getting referred. See our Dental Practice Front-Desk Checklist for the full referral-process audit framework.

Pre-op and post-op communication

The around-the-surgery work AI handles.

Implant patients ask the same 8–10 questions in the days before surgery. 'Should I eat before?' 'What about my blood thinner?' 'How long will I be in chair?' 'Can someone drive me home?' 'When can I exercise again?'

AI handles these consistently — same answer, same depth, same warmth, every time. Your team's bandwidth is freed for the truly clinical questions that need a doctor's voice.

Post-op is similar. Standard post-op instructions go out via SMS automatically. Pain check-ins at day 1, 3, and 7 happen on schedule. If a patient reports pain or swelling outside the expected range, the system escalates to a clinician — not to a generic 'we'll have someone call you back.'

What's not solved yet

What AI in implant practice still can't do.

Three things AI front office still can't (and shouldn't) do for implant practices. First, the case-acceptance conversation — the moment when your treatment coordinator or surgeon walks the patient through the full plan, financing, and timeline. That belongs to humans. Aria sets it up; your team closes it.

Second, the clinical decision: whether this patient is a good candidate, whether they need bone graft first, whether single-stage or two-stage protocol fits. Clinical decisions are clinical, full stop.

Third, the difficult conversations: complications, dissatisfaction, refunds. AI can route them, but the conversation itself should be human. We've written about this balance of human and automation in dental tools; the implant context is one of the most extreme examples of where the line lives.
